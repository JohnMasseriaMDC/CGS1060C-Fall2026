CGS 1060C · Data Analytics
Kinds of data
Four pairs that show up on the quiz
Qualitative vs. quantitative
Qualitative
Describes qualities
e.g. How the massage felt
Quantitative
Measured in numbers
e.g. Massage length: 60 min
Discrete vs. continuous
Discrete
Counted, whole numbers
e.g. Clients today: 14
Continuous
Measured, any value
e.g. Temperature: 98.6°F
Nominal vs. ordinal
Nominal
Categories, no order
e.g. Facial, massage, nails
Ordinal
Categories in order
e.g. Poor, fair, good
Structured vs. unstructured
Structured
Fits rows and columns
e.g. Appointments table
Unstructured
Doesn’t fit a table
e.g. Reviews, photos, audio
Count it = discrete. Measure it = continuous. Order matters = ordinal.

First-, second-, and third-party data
Type

Third-party

Primary vs. secondary
Primary: you collect it new (a client survey). Secondary: someone already did (census.gov).
Open data must be…
Available to the public, free to reuse and share, and usable by anyone. e.g. data.gov
CGS 1060C · Data Analytics
Privacy
Anonymized isn’t always anonymous
PII (personally identifiable information) is anything that points to one person.
Name
Social Security number
Email
Phone number
Account numbers
Medical record number
IP address
Anonymization removes or encrypts PII before data is analyzed or shared.
The trap
ZIP code + birth date + sex. None is PII alone.
Together, a well-known study found they could single out about 87% of Americans.
Latanya Sweeney, 2000
Combining “harmless” fields can re-identify people. That’s why health rules remove far more than names.

Choosing a chart
Match the chart to the question
Line
How did it change over time?
Time goes on the x-axis (horizontal)
Bar
How do groups compare?
The value axis starts at zero

Are two things related?
A pattern is not proof of cause

Must total 100%; five slices at most

CGS 1060C · Data Analytics
Misleading charts · 2
Three more ways a chart can mislead
Cherry-picking
Showing only the data that supports your point
A survey chart shows five ratings near 4.5 and leaves out “ease of ordering” at 2.3.
Fix: show all the results
Improper scaling
Pictures scaled in two directions
Double an icon’s height and width and its area grows 4×, not 2×.
Fix: same-size icons, or bars
Too much information
Eleven slices, tiny labels, heavy gridlines
The message gets lost in the clutter.
Fix: group small slices into “Other”
Ask of every chart: Where does the axis start? What’s missing? Do the pictures match the numbers?
