CLARIFICATION 1 · SECURITY
A virus needs a host. A worm doesn't.
Virus
Worm
How it spreads
Attaches itself to a file or program (the host)
A standalone program that copies itself across a network
What it needs from you
Someone opens or runs the infected file
Nothing. It exploits an unpatched flaw
Classic example
Melissa (1999), a Word macro virus
WannaCry (2017), which hit unpatched Windows PCs worldwide
Best defense
Don't open unexpected attachments; leave macros off
Install updates promptly; keep the firewall on
For completeness, a Trojan horse is disguised as something useful and doesn't copy itself at all. "Malware" is the umbrella word for all three.

CLARIFICATION 2 · HARDWARE SECURITY
TPM stands for Trusted Platform Module
A tamper-resistant security chip, or a protected area built into the processor, that creates and guards encryption keys so they can't be copied off the computer.
What it does for you
  • Holds the key for BitLocker / Device encryption, so a stolen drive is unreadable in another PC
  • Protects your Windows Hello PIN, face, and fingerprint sign-in
  • Checks that startup files weren't tampered with before Windows loads
What it is not
Not antivirus. It doesn't store your files or browser passwords. Not "Trusted Protection Module."
Where to see it
Windows Security › Device security › Security processor details. Windows 11 requires TPM 2.0.
On a Mac
Apple's Secure Enclave plays a similar role.

CLARIFICATION 3 · UPDATES
Patch Tuesday is the second Tuesday of the month
Microsoft releases its monthly security updates on the second Tuesday, around 10 a.m. Pacific (1 p.m. Eastern). Urgent fixes can still arrive any day, "out of band."

The worm didn't beat the patch. It beat the people who hadn't installed it.

READ THIS FIRST · THE HIDDEN STRUCTURE
It predicts the next word. It doesn't look facts up.
Why it's confidently wrong
A wrong answer that sounds right is called a hallucination. Asked for a source, it may invent one that looks real.
Fluent is not the same as correct. You are the fact-check step.

WRITING PROMPTS
Six ingredients of a strong prompt
1 · Be specific
"a 150-word welcome email," not "an email"
2 · Provide context
"for new clients of a small day spa in Doral"
3 · Set a persona
"Act as an experienced spa manager."
4 · Specify the output
"as a table with three columns"
5 · Language and style
"friendly, plain English, short sentences"
6 · Talk naturally
"Write it the way you'd ask a coworker."
Combining techniques just means stacking several ingredients in one prompt.

USING AI RESPONSIBLY
Four responsibilities that come with the tool
Honesty
Follow each course's AI rules and say when you used AI. Passing off AI work as your own is an academic integrity issue.
Privacy
Keep personal, medical, financial, and customer data out of tools your school or employer hasn't approved.
Fairness
Models learn from human writing, stereotypes included. Read output for who is left out or misrepresented.
Ownership
You're responsible for what you submit or send, errors included. "The AI wrote it" isn't a defense.
CGS 1060C · Connect: Using AI Responsibly · Ethical Considerations · SimNet SLO 4.1
VERIFYING AI OUTPUT
Check anything you'd hate to get wrong
  1. Flag the checkable claims: names, numbers, dates, quotes, laws.
  2. Find each one in a source you trust, ideally the company, agency, or publisher itself.
  3. Open every citation. Does it exist, and does it actually say that?
  4. Slow down for recent events. The model may not know about them.

EDITING AND CITING
Make it yours, then say where it came from
Edit to your voice
  • Cut filler and phrases you'd never say
  • Add your own examples and course details
  • Check it answers the actual assignment
  • Read it aloud; if it doesn't sound like you, rewrite it
Cite or disclose
APA-style pattern for an AI tool:
Microsoft. (2026). Copilot [Large language model]. https://copilot.microsoft.com/
Plus one sentence on how you used it: "I used Copilot to brainstorm an outline; the writing is my own."
Follow the format your instructor asks for. APA and MLA both publish current guidance for citing AI.

RESEARCH · STAYING CURRENT · WORK
Three habits that outlast any one tool
Research with integrity
Use AI to find angles and search terms, then read the real sources. Your argument and citations come from them, not the chatbot.
Stay current
Tools change monthly. Follow one or two reliable sources, like the vendor's own blog, and skip the hype. Expect screens to look different from this deck.
Meet workplace expectations
Use approved tools only, keep company and customer data out of public ones, label AI-assisted work when asked, and own the result.
