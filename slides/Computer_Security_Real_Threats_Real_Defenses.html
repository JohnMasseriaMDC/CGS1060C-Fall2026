Threat, vulnerability, exploit, breach
Threat
Anything that could cause a loss of data, money, or service.
Burglars exist in your city.
Vulnerability
A weakness: an unpatched app, a reused password, a careless click.
You left a window unlocked.
Exploit
The specific method that uses the weakness.
Someone climbs in through that window.
Breach
Unauthorized access actually happened.
They're inside your house.
Threat mitigation works on three fronts:
Prevent
Patch, lock, train. Close the window.
Identify
Antivirus, alerts, logs. The alarm goes off.
Cure
Remove, restore, recover. Fix it and clean up.
CGS 1060C · Computer Security
Foundation
The CIA triad: what each attack breaks
C
Confidentiality
Only the right people can see it.
Attack: a packet sniffer on café Wi-Fi; a data breach.
Defense: encryption, strong passwords, MFA.
I
Integrity
Nobody changed it without permission.
Attack: altered bank details on an emailed invoice; malware editing files.
Defense: backups, checksums, version history.
A
Availability
It works when you need it.
Attack: a DDoS flood; ransomware locking files.
Defense: backups, redundancy, updates, UPS.
Ransomware hits two letters at once: it locks your files (A) and threatens to publish them (C).

CGS 1060C · Computer Security
Hidden gem · Microsoft Office
Office's yellow and red bars protect you
A macro virus is code hidden inside a Word or Excel file. It runs when the macro runs, so Office stops it before that happens.
Yellow bar: files from email or the web open read-only in a sandbox. Enable Editing takes the file out of the sandbox.
Red bar: since 2022, Office blocks macros in files from the internet by default.
Rule: never click Enable Content because a document tells you to. That instruction is the attack.
What it looks like (simplified)
PROTECTED VIEW · This file came from the internet. It's safer to stay read-only unless you need to edit.
Enable Editing
SECURITY RISK · Macros in this file were blocked because it came from an untrusted source.

Malware that matters
Ransomware: your backup must be offline
How an attack unfolds
1
Arrives as a phishing attachment, a Trojan, or an unpatched flaw.
2
Quietly encrypts files, including any drive that's plugged in.
3
A note demands payment, usually in cryptocurrency.
4
Often threatens to publish the stolen files too.
What actually saves you
3-2-1 backups: 3 copies, on 2 kinds of storage, with 1 kept offline or offsite.
Sync is not backup. OneDrive and iCloud copy encrypted files too, but version history can roll them back.
Unplug the backup drive when it isn't backing up.
The FBI advises against paying. There's no guarantee you'll get your files back, and payment funds the next attack.

CGS 1060C · Computer Security
Social engineering · Skill
Read a link right to left
The rule: find the first single / after https://. The name just before it is the owner. Everything to its left can be anything the scammer wants.
https://login.microsoftonline.com/
Real: Microsoft's real sign-in domain
https://microsoft.com.account-verify.co/login
Fake: owned by account-verify.co
https://rnicrosoft.com/security
Fake: r + n, not m
Check before you click: on a computer, hover over the link. On a phone, press and hold it.

Social engineering
Red flags that still work, and what to do
Red flags that still work
  • Urgency or threats: “24 hours,” “account closed”
  • Unusual payment: gift cards, crypto, wire transfer
  • Secrecy: “keep this between us”
  • Unexpected link, attachment, or code
  • Sender's domain doesn't match the organization
Spelling mistakes used to be a giveaway. AI now writes polished scams.
What to do instead
  • Don't click. Open the app or type the address yourself.
  • Call back on a number you already have.
  • Use your mail app's Report phishing button.
  • Forward scam texts to 7726 (S-P-A-M).
  • Clicked and typed a password? Change it now and turn on MFA.
CGS 1060C · Computer Security
Social engineering
Deepfakes: seeing and hearing aren't proof
A deepfake is AI-generated audio, photo, or video of a real person doing or saying something they never did.
Legitimate uses: film effects, dubbing into other languages, restoring a voice for someone who lost theirs.
Harmful uses: fraud calls, fake executive video meetings, fake intimate images, misinformation.
Glitches like odd hands or lip-sync errors are getting rarer. Verify the channel, not the pixels.
Defenses that work
  • Agree on a family code word for emergencies.
  • Hang up and call back on a number you know.
  • Urgency + money + secrecy = stop and verify.
  • At work, confirm payment requests in a second channel.

Defenses that actually work
Most of what you just saw is stopped by five habits:

CGS 1060C · Computer Security
Accounts
Length beats complexity
Weak · meets every old rule
P@ssw0rd1!
Predictable swaps; it's in every cracking list.
Strong · four random words
blue taco umbrella saturday
Long and easy to remember. Length is what makes it hard to crack.
What NIST now tells websites (2025)
  • At least 15 characters when a password is the only protection
  • No forced symbol-and-number rules
  • No forced changes every 90 days; change only after a breach
  • Check new passwords against lists of breached passwords
Why no forced changes? People just do Spring2026! → Summer2026!

CGS 1060C · Computer Security
Devices
Antivirus: a wanted poster and a guard
Signature-based: the wanted poster
Compares files to a list of known malware fingerprints.
Strength: fast and accurate for known threats.
Weakness: blind to brand-new malware, so definitions update daily.
Heuristic / behavioral: the guard
Watches for suspicious traits and actions, like a program encrypting hundreds of files.
Strength: catches new and fileless malware.
Weakness: sometimes flags safe files (a false positive).
Built in: Windows Security (Microsoft Defender) on Windows, XProtect on Mac. Run one real-time antivirus, not two.
Scareware: a browser pop-up saying “Your PC is infected, call this number” is the attack.

Devices
Updates close the window attackers use
1 · Flaw found
Researchers or criminals discover a vulnerability.
2 · Patch released
The vendor ships an update that fixes it.
3 · Patch studied
Attackers compare old and new code to find the hole.
4 · Unpatched attacked
Devices without the update become easy targets.
The day a patch comes out, the clock starts for every device that hasn't installed it. Turn on automatic updates, and restart when asked: many updates finish only on restart.
Spectre and Meltdown (2018)
Flaws in the processor chips themselves, fixed through operating system and firmware updates.

Devices
Your router is already a firewall
Internet strangers
Your router 203.0.113.7
Laptop 192.168.1.20
Phone 192.168.1.21
Smart TV 192.168.1.22
NAT lets every device share the router's one public address. Outsiders see only the router, so unrequested traffic has nowhere to go.
Firewall rules check where traffic comes from, where it's going, and which port it uses.
Firewall ≠ antivirus. The firewall guards the door; antivirus checks what's already inside.

What it does
What it doesn't do

CGS 1060C · Computer Security
Optional · Physical security
Physical damage is still the top risk
Drops and spills
The most common way devices actually die. Cases, lids, and no drinks next to the laptop.
Heat
Keep vents clear. A laptop on a bed or blanket can overheat.
Power surges
A surge protector, not just a power strip. Check for a joule rating on the label.
UPS
A battery backup that keeps a desktop running long enough to save and shut down during an outage.
Traveling abroad
Look for INPUT 100–240V on the charger. If it's there, you only need a plug adapter.
